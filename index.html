Strange ideas that changed science.

A short journey through some of science's most fascinating theories — from curved spacetime and quantum particles to hidden dimensions, dark matter and the possibility of other universes.

The idea

Science becomes interesting when reality stops behaving normally.

Some scientific theories describe things that feel impossible at first: clocks running at different speeds, particles influencing one another across enormous distances, invisible matter surrounding galaxies, or even dimensions we cannot see. This page gives you a short introduction to some of those ideas without going too deeply into the mathematics.

Space & Time

Relativity

Einstein's theory of relativity tells us that space and time are not separate fixed things. Together they form spacetime, which can stretch, curve and behave differently depending on speed and gravity.

Simple example

A clock travelling extremely fast through space would tick slightly slower than a clock remaining on Earth. This effect is called time dilation.

02 / 12
Quantum Physics

Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics describes nature at extremely small scales. At this level, particles do not always behave like tiny solid objects. Instead, their properties can be described by probabilities until they are measured.

Simple example

An electron can behave as though several possible paths exist at once, with a measurement determining which result we actually observe.

03 / 12
Quantum Physics

Quantum Entanglement

Entanglement occurs when quantum particles become connected in such a way that measurements of their properties are strongly correlated, even when the particles are separated by enormous distances.

Strange idea

Imagine two quantum particles created together and later taken far apart. Measurements made on one can show correlations with measurements made on the other that ordinary classical physics cannot explain.

04 / 12
Gravity

Black Holes

A black hole is a region where spacetime has been curved so strongly that beyond a boundary called the event horizon, nothing — including light — can escape back out.

Simple example

Near a black hole, gravity affects the passage of time. To a distant observer, a clock closer to the black hole would appear to run more slowly.

05 / 12
Theoretical Physics

Wormholes

Wormholes are hypothetical tunnels through spacetime. Certain mathematical solutions to Einstein's equations allow structures resembling shortcuts between distant regions of space.

Think of it like this

Imagine two distant points drawn on a sheet of paper. Folding the paper could bring those points close together. A wormhole is sometimes described using a similar analogy.

06 / 12
Cosmology

Dark Matter

Galaxies appear to contain more gravitational influence than their visible stars and gas can explain. Scientists call the unseen source responsible for much of this extra gravity dark matter.

Evidence

Stars near the edges of galaxies often orbit faster than expected if only visible matter were producing gravity.

07 / 12
Cosmology

Dark Energy

Observations indicate that the expansion of the universe is accelerating. Dark energy is the name given to whatever is causing or contributing to this large-scale acceleration.

Strange consequence

Very distant galaxies are becoming separated from one another increasingly quickly as cosmic expansion continues.

08 / 12
Speculative Cosmology

Multiverse

The multiverse is a broad family of hypotheses suggesting that our observable universe may be only one part of a much larger collection of universes.

Possible interpretation

Some models of cosmic inflation suggest that different regions of spacetime could develop into separate universe-like regions with different histories.

09 / 12
Fundamental Physics

String Theory

String theory proposes that the most fundamental components of nature may not be point-like particles, but extremely tiny vibrating string-like objects.

Simple analogy

Just as one guitar string can produce different musical notes depending on how it vibrates, different vibrations of fundamental strings could appear as different particles.

10 / 12
Cosmology

Big Bang

The Big Bang model describes the universe as having developed from an extremely hot and dense early state. Space itself has expanded and cooled over billions of years.

Important point

It was not simply an explosion occurring at one location inside empty space. Instead, the distances between regions of space increased as the universe expanded.

11 / 12
Astrobiology

Fermi Paradox

The universe contains an enormous number of stars and planets. If intelligent life is reasonably common, it seems possible that advanced civilizations should already be detectable. Yet so far, we have no confirmed evidence of one.

The question

With billions of potentially suitable worlds in our galaxy alone, why have we not found clear signs of another technological civilization?

12 / 12
Philosophy & Science

Simulation Hypothesis

The simulation hypothesis asks whether a sufficiently advanced civilization could create extremely detailed simulations containing conscious observers — and whether our own reality could theoretically be such a simulation. It remains philosophical and speculative rather than an established scientific theory.

Thought experiment

If future computers could simulate entire worlds whose inhabitants believed their world was real, how could those inhabitants prove whether they were inside a simulation?
